## 5. Four futures

### no change — Fragmented guardrails

National rules, voluntary commitments and multilateral principles continue to multiply without interoperable enforcement. Youth and future-generations concerns appear in language but rarely shape model deployment, procurement or remedy.

### marginal — Standards begin to align

Major jurisdictions converge on shared risk vocabulary, incident reporting and child-safety expectations. Compliance remains uneven, but regulators and standards bodies reduce the most costly gaps between regimes.

### adaptive — Interoperable accountability

Cross-border assurance, independent evaluation and rights-based impact duties become mutually recognised. Public institutions can compare model risks and enforce remedies while youth participation becomes a recurring part of governance design.

### radical — Global duties with remedy

A binding international framework establishes enforceable duties for frontier AI, public-interest access to evidence, and remedies for affected communities. Long-term and intergenerational impacts become explicit tests for deployment.

**What to watch:**
- (adaptive) Two major AI regimes recognise each other's independent assurance results
- (radical) A multilateral agreement creates binding incident disclosure and remedy duties
- (no_change) Competing national standards harden into incompatible compliance blocs
