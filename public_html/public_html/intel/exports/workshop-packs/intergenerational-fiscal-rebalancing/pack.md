## 5. Four futures

> **Projection may be outdated**: trend gained 211 signals since authoring

### no change — Deferral continues

Pension, housing and debt burdens keep shifting quietly onto younger and future taxpayers. Reform is announced and diluted; the political cost of confronting older voter blocs keeps the status quo in place.

### marginal — Trimming at the edges

Retirement ages nudge up, some inheritance and property reliefs narrow, and youth guarantees expand modestly. The direction of travel is fairer but the pace is slow and easily reversed by an electoral cycle.

### adaptive — Structural rebalancing

Several OECD states pair pension reform with serious housing and tax-base changes, framed explicitly as generational fairness. A durable cross-party settlement emerges in at least one country and becomes a reference model.

### radical — Wealth-transfer reset

Inheritance, property and wealth taxation move to the centre of fiscal policy, redistributing accumulated advantage toward younger cohorts. Housing is treated as an intergenerational-justice question, not only an affordability one.

**What to watch:**
- (adaptive) A G7 government passes pension and housing reform under one fairness banner
- (no_change) Organised older-voter mobilisation forces a reform reversal
- (radical) A major economy enacts a standing wealth or inheritance tax rise framed generationally
