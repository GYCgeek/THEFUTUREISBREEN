## 5. Four futures

> **Projection may be outdated**: trend gained 60 signals since authoring

### no change — Consultation theatre

Youth councils and advisory bodies keep multiplying, but real decision power stays elsewhere. Participation is symbolic, episodic and easily ignored when it is inconvenient to those who hold the budget and the vote.

### marginal — Seats at more tables

Youth delegates gain formal presence in more forums and occasional voting rights. Influence remains uneven and personality-dependent, but the expectation that young people are consulted becomes harder to skip openly.

### adaptive — Binding youth checks

Some jurisdictions give youth bodies statutory footing — mandatory consultation, published responses, and youth-impact tests with real procedural teeth. Tokenism becomes politically costly and participation starts to bite on outcomes.

### radical — Shared decision power

Lowered voting ages, co-decision mandates and enforceable youth-representation quotas move young people from advised to deciding. Institutions redesign around durable youth authority rather than time-limited pilots.

**What to watch:**
- (adaptive) A national parliament makes youth-impact consultation legally binding
- (radical) A country lowers its voting age with cross-party support
- (no_change) Flagship youth councils lapse after their founding champions leave office
