## 5. Four futures

> **Projection may be outdated**: trend gained 59 signals since authoring

### no change — Bans without design

Under-16 restrictions and age checks spread as headline measures, but the underlying platform design that drives harm is untouched. Enforcement is thin and workarounds are trivial, so protection is more announced than felt.

### marginal — Patchwork tightening

More countries add age verification and duty-of-care rules, and platforms make selective adjustments. Standards diverge across borders, creating compliance friction without yet forcing a shared safety-by-design baseline.

### adaptive — Safety by design

Regulators converge on design-level obligations — default protections, auditable risk duties, independent oversight — rather than blunt bans. Platforms rebuild youth experiences around these duties and cross-border alignment firms up.

### radical — Structural liability

Binding, enforceable duties with real penalties make platforms liable for foreseeable youth harm. Business models change because the cost of harmful design finally lands on the designer, not the child.

**What to watch:**
- (adaptive) A major bloc adopts enforceable safety-by-design duties over blanket bans
- (radical) A regulator wins a penalty case establishing platform liability for youth harm
- (no_change) Flagship bans prove unenforceable and quietly lapse
