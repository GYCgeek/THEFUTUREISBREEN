## 5. Four futures

> **Projection may be outdated**: trend gained 55 signals since authoring

### no change — Isolated experiments

Future-generations commissioners and councils remain scattered pilots, admired but rarely copied, and vulnerable to defunding. The UN-level ambition stays aspirational without machinery underneath it.

### marginal — Slow replication

The Welsh-style model is adopted by a few more jurisdictions and referenced in UN processes. Mandates vary widely and durability is untested through hostile governments.

### adaptive — A standardising network

A transnational peer network forms among future-generations institutions, converging mandates and giving the model institutional durability. The UN Declaration acquires implementing bodies rather than only text.

### radical — Guardians with teeth

Future-generations institutions gain investigatory power, statutory triggers and standing to challenge decisions. Representing the unborn becomes an enforceable function of the state, not an advisory courtesy.

**What to watch:**
- (adaptive) Future-generations institutions form a standing international network
- (radical) A commissioner gains statutory power to block or delay a decision
- (no_change) An established commissioner is abolished after a change of government
