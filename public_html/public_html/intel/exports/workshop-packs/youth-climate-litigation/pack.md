## 5. Four futures

> **Projection may be outdated**: trend gained 47 signals since authoring

### no change — Two-track stalemate

International law keeps recognising climate duties while domestic courts, especially in the US, keep closing the door on youth standing. The gap between advisory principle and enforceable remedy stays wide.

### marginal — Selective openings

A few state or national courts accept narrow youth climate claims, and the ICJ opinion is cited more often. Wins stay jurisdiction-specific and are met with legislative pushback elsewhere.

### adaptive — Doctrine travels

ICJ-derived duties begin entering domestic merits rulings, and a line of favourable youth cases hardens into citable doctrine. Litigation risk starts shaping government and corporate climate conduct.

### radical — Enforceable duty of care

Courts across multiple jurisdictions recognise an enforceable climate duty owed to the young and unborn, backed by remedies. Litigation becomes a reliable lever, and climate inaction carries hard legal cost.

**What to watch:**
- (adaptive) A domestic merits ruling relies on the ICJ advisory opinion
- (radical) Several jurisdictions recognise an enforceable climate duty to future generations
- (no_change) A supreme court forecloses youth standing and others follow
