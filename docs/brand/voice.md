# GYC voice

## In one line
Specific, confident and warm. We sound like young people who have actually been in the negotiating room and have the receipts, not like a brochure.

## The 2026 frame: The Year of the Sky
Everything the site says in 2026 should sound like Sky: calm authority, competence and structure. Not louder; stronger.
- Use GYC's own lines (facts.yaml → strategy_2026): "Clarity over chaos, structure over spectacle, capability over commentary." "From visibility to credibility." "We are not selling outrage. We are building human infrastructure." "Fire without Sky is instability. Fire after Sky is influence."
- Frame youth as **systems-literate participants**, not symbolic presences. GYCT makes people "procedurally literate"; R4P is "connection infrastructure, not protest infrastructure".
- **No fight language.** Sky says "We are not preparing youth to fight. We are preparing them to outgrow broken systems." Replace "lead the fight against climate change", "battle", "unstoppable force", "relentless" with build / prepare / negotiate / outlast.
- Use the arc when explaining where GYC is going: Ocean (2025) → Sky (2026) → Roots (2027) → Fire (2028).
- Use the Skills Pipeline names for the tracks, and the Avocado Framework as the lens above them.
- Show the decision-making engine honestly: who decides what (facts.yaml → governance.decision_domains). Funders asked for exactly this.

## Principles
1. **Specific beats impressive.** Name the place, the date, the number, the person. "Baku, 2024. Our first GYCT delegation." beats "We empower youth globally."
2. **Proof before promise.** Every claim of impact sits next to evidence: a number from facts.yaml, a named partner, a photo, a document.
3. **Changemakers, not activists.** We build, train, negotiate and research. We do not rant, and we do not perform outrage.
4. **Short sentences. Plain verbs. Sentence case.** No ALL CAPS paragraphs, no exclamation marks in body copy.
5. **Buttons say what happens.** "Read the 2025 report", "Apply to GYCT", "Write to the team". Never "Learn more >>", "Click here", "Submit".
6. **Honest about limits.** "GYC does not issue badges or fund travel." Saying what we don't do builds trust with funders and applicants.

## Our own bad lines (remove on sight)
- "Welcome to Our Vibrant Website! Embark on an Exciting Journey… Every Click Unveils Something New." → template filler.
- "We Deliver High End Level Results" → empty superlative.
- "Our Partners Rock!" → not how funders read.
- "WE'VE TRAINED YOUNGSTERS FROM 178 COUNTRIES. WE FELT NOT TO HAVE THE CAPACITY…" → shouting, unclear.
- "Unleashing the Power… Revolutionary Program… nothing short of revolutionary" → hype words.
- "Read more >>", "Learn more", "See All Options" → vague signposts.
- "lead the fight against climate change" (homepage vision) and "We're an unstoppable force… relentless" (homepage values) → pre-Sky activist register.

## Words
- Prefer: train, negotiate, build, publish, fund, host, deliver, research, convene.
- Avoid: revolutionary, unleash, empower (as a filler verb), vibrant, journey, dream team, rock, geeks, high-end, fight, battle, unstoppable, relentless.
- Reach vs depth: never blur them. "100,000+ views of recorded sessions" (reach) is different from "5,400 young people trained" (depth). Funders explicitly asked GYC to separate the two.
- Numbers: "5,400 young people trained" (number first, plain noun, no "+" unless the source uses it).
- Dates: "9–20 November 2026". Times in UTC with local in brackets where relevant.
- Spelling: British English (GYC is Swiss-registered, works with Oxford, and the GYCT page already uses British spelling).

## Tone by audience
- Funders: calm, evidential, short. Lead with outcomes and governance.
- Applicants: direct and encouraging. Lead with dates, eligibility, the one next step.
- Press: one paragraph, credentials, a contact.
