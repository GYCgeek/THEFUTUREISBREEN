# Who we want to look like

We are not copying anyone. We are taking one specific lesson from each reference. Visit the live sites, study them, then apply the lesson in GYC's own visual language.

## The blend
**charity: water** for proof, **Atmos** for culture, **Our World in Data** for rigour.

### 1. charity: water — proof and money transparency (primary)
- Lesson: every promise is next to evidence, and the site openly says where money goes. People are photographed doing the work, not posing.
- Why GYC needs it: funders are now the first audience, and the January 2026 grant reviewers flagged measurable outcomes and financial durability. The site should answer "is this real and will it last?" without anyone asking.
- Apply to: homepage proof band, a "How we're funded" section (wording approved by a human), Youth House partner tiers, impact reports.

### 2. Atmos — youth climate culture with editorial taste
- Lesson: climate shown through people, places and culture, with large confident photography and generous space. Feels young without feeling like a student project.
- Why GYC needs it: GYC's strongest assets are its people and places (the Tree of Hope in the Blue Zone, the Amazon river journey, Champs from 12 countries). Today they're buried in carousels.
- Apply to: About ("Our story, by room"), the Champs gallery, Youth House, R4P.

### 3. Our World in Data — rigour in every number
- Lesson: each figure has a source one click away; charts are clean and honest.
- Why GYC needs it: the site currently shows the same metric three different ways (60K vs 100K views, $300K vs $350K, 170+ vs 178 countries). Sourced, single-valued numbers are cheap credibility.
- Apply to: every number on the site (pull from facts.yaml, show source), GYCT impact, Foresight research.

## How the Sky concept should (and should not) look
The booklet says it directly: "The sky is not decoration. It is not branding. It is not aesthetic language. It is a discipline of altitude."
- **Do not** illustrate Sky with clouds, sky-blue gradients, birds or sunrise stock photos. That would be the exact decoration the concept rejects.
- **Do** express altitude as clarity: generous space, overview before detail, one clear map of how things connect (Avocado lens → Skills Pipeline → programmes → outcomes), a calm and consistent grid, and fewer, larger elements.
- The Ocean → Sky → Roots → Fire arc is a real sequence, so a numbered or timeline treatment is legitimate there (and only there).
- The governance model is a real system: a clean, redrawn public version of the Sky Governance Model diagram is a strong credibility asset. The internal draft (in the GYC Drive, not in this repo) is too dense to publish as is.

## GYC's own visual anchors (use these before inventing anything)
- The 2026 Champs poster portraits (`/assets/images/champs/2026/`) — the strongest current visual system on the site.
- The Avocado Framework illustration and the animated film in production (visual style locked; model sheets exist for the house, the avocado character and the industrial hands). Ask for these assets in QUESTIONS — the website should feel like the same family as the film.
- Real photography from COP27–COP30, the Tapajós river journey, UNOC Nice.

## What we must NOT look like
- A generic NGO template: hero slider, three icon cards, "Our Mission / Our Vision / Our Values" blocks, stock photos.
- A UN agency PDF dump: walls of text, links to documents instead of content.
- The current AI-generated look: cream background with terracotta accent, or near-black with one acid-green accent; ALL-CAPS eyebrow labels over every heading; "01 / 02 / 03" numbering on things that aren't steps; identical rounded cards with soft shadows; fade-up animation on every section.
- A startup landing page. GYC is an institution in the making, not a product launch.

## Principles for the design-critic
- One memorable thing per page; everything around it quiet.
- Real people and real places over illustration and icons.
- Every section earns its place. If removing it loses nothing, remove it.
- Mobile first: most applicants are on phones in MAPA countries on slow connections. Pages must be fast (compressed images, no autoplay video, no 30 MB downloads without a size warning).
- Accessible: contrast AA minimum, visible focus, alt text that describes, reduced motion respected.
